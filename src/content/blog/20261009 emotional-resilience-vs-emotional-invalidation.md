title: "Emotional Resilience vs Emotional Invalidation"
date: 2026-10-09
tags: ["journal"]

There's a tool that I developed early on in life that served me well for a long time. Whenever something affected me, I would dismiss the emotion that came attached to it. 

After a while, that tool becomes reflexive, and feels mighty useful because you can turn a stoic cheek to anything that bothers you. In fact, there's a manliness to it that I came to take pride in. I remember college peers came to admire it too, e.g. "wow he's so serious and stoic".   

As I've gotten older, that tool started to lose its effectiveness. Dismissed emotions found a way of butting their head back into present day with increased ferocity. Time I spent revisiting past events increased and greater the sense became that my life was built on a house of cards. 

There's key people I came across during this time of insecurity that gave me a glimpse into what identifying and processing emotions meant; a seed was planted. 

Nowadays, I find myself increasingly trying to sit with uncomfortable emotions, but it took years to get to this point. In the early stages, I would recall a painful event from my life and feel a twang of sadness for the way it had altered my trajectory, but I would immediately stop myself from inhabiting the feeling. To truly sit with the feeling felt performative. I felt like a fraud. 

_Why are you vying for attention? You've had privileges others can only dream of. Don't forget how lucky and privileged you are you ****ing dumbass. Things could be way worse. You know who talks about emotions? People who can't deal. Pussies._

And what I have arrived at more recently is a distinction between emotional resilience and emotional invalidation. 

**Resilience** means being able to acknowledge that something hurt, carry that knowledge, and continue living your life. **Invalidation** means insisting that it shouldn't have hurt in the first place, and what follows is that part of you becomes attached/stuck/frozen to that time in your life. 

You never process it.
